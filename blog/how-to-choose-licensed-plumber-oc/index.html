Hiring a Plumber

How to Choose a Licensed Plumber in Orange County: A Verification Checklist

By Viper Rooter & Plumbing · September 30, 2026 · 5 min read

Before you hire a plumber in California, you can verify most of what matters in a few minutes, and this checklist walks through it in order. Start by confirming the contractor holds an active state license, which you can check at the Contractors State License Board. From there, get the scope and price in writing, compare more than one bid on what each actually covers, and confirm insurance. Viper Rooter & Plumbing’s license number is 1022901, and you can run the same check on us that this guide recommends for anyone. Below is each step, what it confirms, and the warning signs that should slow you down.

Verify the license at CSLB, step by step

This is the first step because it filters out unlicensed operators and costs only a minute.

Go to the Contractors State License Board website at cslb.ca.gov, or call the board at 800-321-2752. Use the license check tool and enter the contractor’s license number. The record shows whether the license is active, what classification it covers, whether the contractor carries workers’ compensation coverage, and whether there are disciplinary actions on file. An active license in the right classification, with workers’ comp and no open discipline, is what you are confirming.

Do this before you hire, and for every bid you take seriously, not only the one you are leaning toward. The check is public and fast, and any licensed plumber expects you to run it. You can try the tool now with our number, 1022901, to see how the lookup works before you are comparing bids.

The warning signs that should slow you down

Once the license checks out, a few signs still tell you something about who you are dealing with. Any one of these is a reason for caution, and several together is a reason to stop.

A fake, expired, or unverifiable license number is the clearest. A number that does not match the name on the CSLB record, or a contractor who will not provide one, has answered the question for you.

“I work under someone else’s license” is another. The person doing your work should be covered by a valid license, not borrowing one.

Cash-only pressure, or a heavy push for full payment up front, appears frequently in plumbing complaints, and so does refusing to put an estimate in writing. A licensed contractor provides a written estimate before starting and will commit the scope and price to writing.

Get it in writing, and compare more than one bid

Two habits protect you more than almost anything else: get the scope and price in writing, and compare more than one bid.

A written estimate pins down what you are paying for and gives you something to hold the final bill against. A contractor confident in their pricing will put it in writing; resistance to doing so is a sign in itself.

More than one bid gives you a reference range for your job, so an inflated quote and a suspiciously low one both stand out. A substantially lower bid deserves a closer look rather than an automatic yes. Compare the scope, the materials, the permit requirements, the warranty, the exclusions, and whether the contractor is properly licensed and insured before assuming the lowest number represents the same job. When you are comparing camera-inspection or drain-special offers, the same rule applies, and our posts on what the $89 drain cleaning covers and how to read your sewer camera inspection walk through how to compare those.

What licensed, insured, and family-owned each tell you

These three phrases are often used as marketing, so here is what each one confirms and what it does not.

Licensed means the state has verified the contractor’s qualifications and holds them to a standard, and it gives you a record and a board to turn to if something goes wrong. It is checkable, which is the point.

Insured means that if property is damaged or someone is hurt during the work, the coverage protects you rather than leaving the cost on you. Confirm both the license and the insurance.

Family-owned describes the business. It is not a substitute for checking the license, the insurance, the written scope, the reviews, the warranty, and the permit practices. Treat it as a descriptor and verify the items on this list regardless.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check a plumber’s license in California?

Go to cslb.ca.gov or call the Contractors State License Board at 800-321-2752, and enter the license number. The record shows whether the license is active, its classification, workers’ comp coverage, and any disciplinary history. Check every serious bid, not just one.

What license number does Viper hold?

Viper Rooter & Plumbing holds California license number 1022901, and we are licensed and insured. It is published so you can verify it, which is what this checklist recommends for any contractor.

What questions should I ask a plumber before hiring?

Ask for the license number and verify it, ask whether they carry insurance, ask for a written estimate before work starts, and ask what the price includes. How readily they answer tells you as much as the answers.

Is the cheapest plumber usually the best deal?

Not reliably. A bid well below the others may reflect a narrower scope, cheaper materials, skipped permits, or a missing credential. Compare scope, materials, permits, warranty, and licensing before assuming the lowest number covers the same job.

What does it mean if a plumber will not give a written estimate?

It is a reason for caution. A licensed contractor will put the scope and price in writing before starting, so resistance to a written estimate is a warning sign.
