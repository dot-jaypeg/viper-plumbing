Is This a Plumbing Emergency? What to Do Right Now

By Viper Rooter & Plumbing · September 30, 2026 · 5 min read

If water is actively flooding, sewage is backing up into the house, you have no water at all, or you smell gas, that is a real plumbing emergency. Do this first. Shut off your water at the main valve, either where the line enters the house or at the street meter. If the water heater is the source, shut it off too. If you smell gas, get everyone out of the house first, then call the gas utility from outside. Once the water is off, call a 24/7 plumber at (657) 637-8529. Shutting the main is the single most important thing you can do to stop the damage while help is on the way. Everything below explains how, and it also tells you when what you have can safely wait for regular hours so you are not paying emergency rates for a slow drip.

The first five minutes: shut the water off

Most of the damage in a plumbing emergency happens in the minutes before the water is stopped, so this comes before anything else.

Find your main shutoff. In most Orange County homes it is in one of three places: on an exterior wall where the water line enters (often near the front hose bib), inside the garage, or at the street in a covered meter box near the curb. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops, or turn a lever valve a quarter turn so it sits crosswise to the pipe. It is worth walking outside right now, on a calm day, and finding yours before you ever need it. Knowing where that valve is can be the difference between a mopped floor and a flooded one.

Shut the water heater if it is the leak. If the flooding is coming from the tank itself, close the cold-water valve on top of the heater. For a gas heater, turn the gas control to off. For an electric heater, switch off its breaker. That stops the tank from refilling and spilling.

For a gas smell, leave first. If you smell rotten eggs or sulfur, do not flip switches, light anything, or hunt for the source. Get everyone outside, then call the gas utility from outside the home, and after that call a licensed plumber. Gas is the one emergency where leaving the house comes before any shutoff you would do yourself.

What actually counts as an emergency

Not every plumbing problem needs someone out at 2 a.m., and knowing which is which saves you money.

These are real emergencies: active flooding you cannot stop, sewage backing up into tubs or floor drains, a burst or major leaking pipe, no water in the whole house, a water heater leaking heavily, or any gas smell. These get worse by the minute and can damage the structure, so they are worth an immediate call at any hour.

These usually are not emergencies: a single slow drain, a dripping faucet, a running toilet, or one fixture that is out while the rest of the house works fine. They are annoying, and they should get fixed, but they can wait for normal hours, and waiting will cost you less. We will tell you that on the phone rather than book an after-hours visit you did not need.

The gray-area cases are the ones to shut off and then decide. A leak under a sink you can isolate with the local shutoff, or a toilet you can stop at its own valve, buys you time to call during regular hours. If you cannot isolate it and water keeps coming, treat it as an emergency and shut the main.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is my main water shutoff valve?

Usually on an exterior wall where the water line enters the house, in the garage, or at the street in a covered meter box near the curb. Turn a round valve clockwise until it stops, or a lever valve a quarter turn crosswise to the pipe. Find yours before you need it.

Is a leaking pipe always an emergency?

No. If you can stop it at a local shutoff or the main and the water is contained, it can often wait for regular hours at a lower cost. It is an emergency when you cannot stop the water or it is actively flooding or reaching the structure.

What should I do first if I smell gas?

Leave the house with everyone in it. Do not flip switches or light anything. Call the gas utility from outside, then call a licensed plumber. Gas is the one case where getting out comes before any shutoff.
