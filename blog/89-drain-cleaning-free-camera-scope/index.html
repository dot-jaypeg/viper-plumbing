What Our $89 Drain Cleaning and Free Camera Scope Really Catches (and the Fine Print, in Writing)

By Viper Rooter & Plumbing · September 30, 2026 · 5 min read

You have seen the cheap drain-cleaning special before, and if you are a little suspicious of it, you are right to be. So here is ours in plain writing, before you ever pick up the phone. Our $89 drain cleaning includes a free camera scope. The cleaning clears a standard accessible drain, and the camera then shows what is actually going on inside the line: roots, grease, a bellied section, an offset joint, or a crack, the kinds of problems a cable cannot fix. A snake clears a soft clog. When the camera shows roots or heavy buildup, the real fix is hydro jetting or a repair, and we will show you the footage before we ever quote it. The point of the free scope is that you find the true cause instead of paying to clear a symptom that comes right back.

The fine print, spelled out

The reason cheap drain specials have a bad name is that the fine print usually hides where the price stops. So here is where ours stands.

The $89 covers cleaning a standard, accessible drain line through a proper cleanout. It is a real service that resolves a lot of common clogs on the spot. What it is not is a blank check to fix anything the camera finds. If the scope reveals a collapsed pipe, a heavy root intrusion, or a line that needs jetting rather than snaking, that is additional work at a quoted price, and you decide whether to do it. We tell you that here, in advance, because a special that pretends to cover every possible problem is the setup for the bait-and-switch you are worried about.

The free camera scope is not a hook to manufacture expensive work. It is there so that when we do recommend more, you can see with your own eyes why. No footage, no upsell.

The scam you are bracing for, and how we refuse it

Let us name the trap directly, because you already know it exists. The classic drain-special scam works two ways. First, the advertised price quietly covers only a short run of a secondary line, so the moment your actual clog is anywhere else, the price jumps. Second, and worse, some outfits run a camera and show you alarming footage of a broken pipe that may not even be yours, then use it to sell a replacement you do not need.

Here is how we make sure that cannot happen with us. You watch the camera screen live, in real time, while we run it. Before we go down the line, we can film something identifiable at your own cleanout so you know the footage is your pipe and not a stock clip. And we do not recommend a repair off footage you did not see. If a company will not let you watch the screen or hand you a copy, that is the tell, and it is exactly the behavior our free scope is built to be the opposite of.

Snake, jet, or repair: what each one is actually for

Knowing the three levels keeps you from overpaying and from underfixing.

A snake, or cable machine, spins a head down the line and punches through a soft clog. It is the right tool for a simple, one-time blockage, and it is what the $89 cleaning uses on a standard drain. A snake does not remove grease coating the pipe wall or a root mass, and it does not fix a damaged pipe.

Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full inside wall of the pipe, cutting roots and blasting away grease and scale. It lasts far longer than a snake for buildup and roots because it cleans the wall instead of boring a hole. It costs more than the $89 cleaning because it is a bigger job, and we only recommend it when the camera shows a cause a snake will not hold against.

A repair, whether a spot dig or a liner, is for a pipe that is cracked, collapsed, offset, or bellied. No amount of cleaning fixes a broken pipe. When the camera shows that, we show you, and we quote the repair.

When the cheap fix is the right call

Here is the flip side. If your clog is a simple, accessible blockage and the camera comes back clean, the $89 cleaning is the whole fix and you are done. We are not going to invent a reason to jet a line that does not need it, and we will tell you when a snake solved it.

Cutting the corner cuts the other way too. If the camera clearly shows roots or a bellied line and you decline the real fix to save money today, the clog comes back and you pay to clear it again, and again. Over a year, that repeat clearing usually costs more than the one-time repair would have.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the $89 drain cleaning a real price or a teaser?

It is a real price for cleaning a standard, accessible drain through a cleanout, and it includes the free camera scope. If the camera finds a problem a cleaning cannot fix, that additional work is quoted separately and you approve it first. We put that in writing up front.

What does the free camera scope actually show?

It shows the inside of your line so you can see roots, grease buildup, bellies, offsets, and cracks. You watch the screen live, and you can get a copy. It is how you confirm the real cause instead of guessing.

Why is my drain still clogged after it was snaked?

Because a snake clears the blockage in the way but not the cause behind it, like grease on the wall or roots at a joint. If a drain re-clogs after snaking, the camera usually shows why, and hydro jetting or a repair is the durable fix.

Will you try to upsell me during the special?

We only recommend more work when the camera shows a problem a cleaning cannot fix, and we show you that footage first. If the scope is clean, the $89 cleaning is the whole job.

Can I get a copy of my camera footage?

Yes. You watch it live while we run it, and you can have a copy. Being able to keep the footage is part of how you can confirm the recommendation for yourself.
